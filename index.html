Yes — real work, worth naming honestly. The point isn't that the jobs don't matter. It's that each one can now be done directly, with software, or by the professional the law always required anyway.

The practical part

How to sell your home without a realtor in Alberta.

The whole process, start to keys. None of it requires a licensed agent — the last step requires a lawyer, and that's the one you keep.

01

Price it on real comps

Pull recent sales of similar homes nearby and set a realistic range. Price is the biggest lever — get it close and the rest follows.

02

Prepare the home and the paperwork

Clean, declutter, get good photos. Order or locate a current Real Property Report with compliance so title can transfer cleanly.Alberta · RPR + compliance

03

List it — direct, MLS, or both

Post it where buyers look. A flat-fee mere posting gets you on MLS without full commission; a direct marketplace reaches buyers searching your area. No exclusivity required.

04

Show it safely

Verify buyers are pre-approved before they visit, keep valuables secured, and don't show alone. Have your answers — roof, furnace, taxes, condo fees — ready.

05

Handle offers and conditions

Use a proper written purchase contract, counter in writing, and track every condition — financing, inspection, deadlines — so nothing slips.Alberta · written purchase contract

06

Close with a lawyer

Your lawyer handles the title transfer, trust funds, and closing. The one required professional — and the one you were always going to pay.

Straight answers

The questions people actually ask.

Is it legal to sell my house without a realtor in Alberta?
Yes. There's no law requiring a real estate agent to buy or sell a home in Alberta. The only professional the law requires for the transfer of ownership is a lawyer.
Do I still need a lawyer?
Yes — this is the non-negotiable part. A real estate lawyer handles the title transfer, trust funds, and closing. It's required whether or not an agent is involved, which is exactly why skipping the agent doesn't skip the legal safeguard.
Will buyers' agents refuse to show my home if I'm not paying commission?
Some might steer around it — a real consideration. You have options: offer a buyer-agent commission if you choose (your decision, not a rule), or reach buyers who aren't working with an agent at all, which is where direct marketplaces come in.
Can I still get on MLS?
Yes. A mere posting through a listing brokerage puts you on MLS for a flat fee, without full-service commission. You can list there and direct-to-buyer at the same time — there's no exclusivity requirement.
Isn't doing it myself risky and complicated?
The hard part was never the individual steps — it was coordinating them without help. That coordination is what software now handles: pricing data, offer tracking, condition deadlines, and a lawyer for the close. The risk drops when the process is guided.
