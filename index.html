Do you legally need a realtor in Alberta?
No.

Only a lawyer is required to close a home sale in Alberta. An agent never was — it was just the default. Here's what the law actually requires, what the assumption costs you, and how to sell your home yourself.

01What the law requires

One of these is the law. The other is a habit.

Alberta law requires a lawyer to transfer title — not an agent. Everything else about using a realtor is convention, not a legal requirement.

Actually required
A real estate lawyer to transfer title and close the deal
A valid written purchase contract both sides sign
Usually a current Real Property Report with compliance
Honest disclosure of what you know about the home
Assumed, not required
A listing agent to sell your home
A buyer's agent on the other side

03A fair question

"But doesn't a realtor actually do things?"

Yes — real work, worth naming honestly. The point isn't that the jobs don't matter. It's that each one can now be done directly, with software, or by the professional the law always required.

Pricing the home

An agent brings a market analysis. Without one: you set the price from what similar homes nearby have sold and listed for, and an appraiser can give you an independent number for a flat fee.

Paperwork & closing

The part with real legal weight. Now: it's handled by your own lawyer — required regardless of whether an agent was ever involved.

05Straight answers

The questions people actually ask.

Plain-English answers about selling and buying a home in Alberta without an agent.

Is it legal to sell my house without a realtor in Alberta?
Yes. There's no law requiring a real estate agent to buy or sell a home in Alberta. The only professional the law requires for the transfer of ownership is a lawyer.
Do I still need a lawyer?
Yes — this is the non-negotiable part. A real estate lawyer handles the title transfer, trust funds, and closing. It's required whether or not an agent is involved, which is exactly why skipping the agent doesn't skip the legal safeguard.
Isn't doing it myself risky and complicated?
The hard part was never the individual steps — it was coordinating them without help. That coordination is what software can now handle: verified buyers, showings, offers and counters in one thread, and every condition deadline tracked — with your own lawyer for the close.
